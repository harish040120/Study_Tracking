## 1. Process Overview (2026 cycle)

Based on multiple 2026 candidate reports, the Amazon SDE Intern (6-month) hiring loop generally looks like this:

1. **Application / Hiring Interest Form** — via Amazon University Talent Acquisition (AUTA), campus drive, HackOn hackathon, LinkedIn/referral, or the Amazon jobs portal.
2. **Online Assessment (OA)** — on HackerRank, ~90–105 minutes:
   - 2 coding questions (Medium–Hard)
   - Work Style / Work Simulation Assessment (situational judgment based on the 16 Leadership Principles)
   - Sometimes an additional "Communication" or MCQ section (OS, CN, OOPs, Linux)
3. **Technical Interview Round(s)** — 1–2 rounds, 45–60 min each, virtual (Zoom/Amazon Chime), shared coding doc:
   - Self-introduction + project deep-dive
   - 1–2 Leadership Principle (behavioral) questions
   - 1–2 DSA coding problems (Medium, occasionally Hard)
4. **(Sometimes) Bar Raiser / Hiring Manager round** — heavier on Leadership Principles and project depth, occasional light system-design discussion.

Key recurring theme across **every** 2026 report: **Leadership Principles are weighted as heavily as, or more heavily than, DSA.** Interviewers deep-dive into behavioral answers with multiple follow-ups.

## 4. Behavioral / Leadership Principle (LP) Questions Reported

These came up repeatedly across 2026 interviews — Amazon's **16 Leadership Principles** underpin all of them (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Invent and Simplify, Are Right a Lot, Learn and Be Curious, Hire and Develop the Best, Insist on the Highest Standards, Think Big, Bias for Action, Frugality, Earn Trust, Dive Deep, Have Backbone/Disagree and Commit, Deliver Results, Strive to be Earth's Best Employer, Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility).

- Why Amazon? Why this internship program?
- Describe a situation where you had to make a quick decision (Bias for Action).
- Tell me about a time you had to work under a tight deadline / a project with a strict deadline.
- Have you taken initiative in any project and made it work? (Ownership)
- Have you ever solved a particularly complex/difficult problem? Walk me through it. (Dive Deep)
- Tell me about a time you communicated a missed deadline to a stakeholder.
- Tell me about one of your learning experiences. (Learn and Be Curious)
- Describe an independent project you completed end-to-end. (Ownership / Deliver Results)
- Explain a project in depth — problem statement, design decisions, your specific contribution, challenges faced, and the impact/metrics. (Interviewers deep-dive relentlessly here — know your numbers.)
- How do you use AI/GenAI tools in your day-to-day work or projects? (Increasingly common in 2026 loops)

## 6. Online Assessment (OA) Structure — What to Expect

- **Format:** HackerRank, 90–105 minutes, proctored.
- **Coding section:** 2 problems, typically Medium or Medium–Hard, covering arrays, strings, graphs, DP, sliding window, and searching/sorting.
- **Work Style / Work Simulation Assessment:** Situational-judgment questions where you pick the "most Amazonian" response — grounded entirely in the 16 Leadership Principles. Stay internally consistent; avoid picking only extreme answers.
- **Optional extra section (seen in some 2026 drives):** MCQs on OS, Computer Networks, OOPs, Linux, or a "Communication" section (choosing the most professional email response).
- Common OA topics reported: two-pointer/greedy pairing, subarray sum parity, subsequence counting, XOR/median subarray counts, rotated sorted array search, string rotation minimization.

## 7. Preparation Checklist

- [ ] Solve 150–250 LeetCode problems, weighted toward **Arrays, Strings, Trees, Graphs (BFS/DFS/Topological Sort), DP, and Heaps** — these dominate the question list above.
- [ ] Specifically drill: LRU/LFU Cache, Linked List manipulation (copy w/ random pointer, merge K lists), Binary Search on answer (ship packages, capacity problems), Top-K / heap problems, Grid DFS/BFS (islands, rotting oranges, water flow).
- [ ] Practice explaining brute-force → optimized approach out loud, including time/space complexity, before writing code (repeatedly emphasized across reports).
- [ ] Prepare 5–8 STAR-format stories covering: Ownership, Bias for Action, Dive Deep, Deliver Results, Learn and Be Curious, and a "conflict/disagreement" story (Have Backbone, Disagree and Commit).
- [ ] Know your resume projects cold — expect deep, multi-layered follow-up questions on design decisions and measurable impact.
- [ ] Skim GenAI basics (embeddings, vector DBs, RAG) if you have any ML/AI content on your resume.
- [ ] Take 2–3 full timed mock OAs (2 coding + work-style survey) in the final two weeks.
